## 2. Basic Terminologies


### 2.1 <u>Subjective Baseline</u>:

- **Consciousness:** Ability to have a default computational mode.
- **Ego:** Ability to differentiate between subject and object.
- **Content:** Information that comes into attention of consciousness.


### 2.2 <u>Sources Of Information</u>:

- **Subject:** A processing function made by combining consciousness and ego.
- **Object:** Everything other the subject.
- **System:** Alternative term used for *object*.


### 2.3 <u>Primitives Types</u>:

- **Rasa:** Emotion
- **Navrasa:** Set of 9 *rasa* that is described in Hindu religious texts.
- **Instinctual variant:** Set of sources which fuels *rasa* (emotion).

## 4. Navrasa / Nine Emotions


### 4.1 <u>Type-1 (Fury)</u>:

*Fury* is also known as *wrath* or *anger* is 1st in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion goes through constant stream of anger coming from a certain source for any given reason. These sources might change or remain the same for long-term.


### 4.2 <u>Type-2 (Love)</u>:

*Love* which is broadly known as *love & beauty* is 2nd in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion has a constant feeling of love, admiration, or strong affinity to something or a person, focused on the feeling of love it evokes in them.


### 4.3 <u>Type-3 (Disgust)</u>:

*Disgust* is 3rd in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion constantly feels disgusted or unsatisfied with someone or something, often pushing them to improve that person or thing for its betterment or in hope to get rid of their disgust.


### 4.4 <u>Type-4 (Sorrow)</u>:

*Sorrow* which is broadly known as *sorrow & compassion*, is 4th in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion has a constant sorrow towards something or someone. Whether they prefer to be in this state or hope to move out depends solely on them.


### 4.5 <u>Type-5 (Wonder)</u>:

*Wonder* is 5th in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion has a constant feeling of awe in things or people. This awe is often curiosity or feeling of amaze towards what they look for, and feeling drives them.


### 4.6 <u>Type-6 (Fear)</u>:

*Fear* is 6th in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion has a constant sense of fear or unpredictability. This feeling might come from various reasons, and could be obsessive like any other emotion from *Navras*.


### 4.7 <u>Type-7 (Mirth)</u>:

*Mirth* which is broadly known as *mirth & laughter* is 7th in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion has a constant sense of fun and laughter. This might come from shared jokes, finding funny details, or genuinely seeking laughter in life.


### 4.8 <u>Type-8 (Bravery)</u>:

*Bravery* is 8th in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion has a constant feeling of bravery or fearlessness. This might come from various reasons including inspiration, personal experiences, and power in literal sense.


### 4.9 <u>Type-9 (Peace)</u>:

*Peace* which is broadly known as *peace & tranquility* is 9th and last in the series of nine emotions. And a subject having habitual orientation to this emotion has a constant feeling of peace and tranquility, as the name says. Their source of tranquility could be spatial, mental, or spiritual.



## 5. Instinctual Variants


### 5.1 <u>Self-Preservation (SP)</u>:

*Self-preservation instinct* is 1st in the series of three instinctual variants. Someone having this as their primary instinct receives fuel to their emotion or *rasa* from themselves. And these personal attributes brought into focus could be positive, negative, or neutral.


### 5.2 <u>Sexual (SX)</u>:

*Sexual instinct* is 2nd in the series of three instinctual variants. Someone having this as their primary instinct receives fuel to their emotion or *rasa* from the immediate spatial object they encounter, whether living or non-living. And these attributes could be positive, negative, or neutral.


### 5.3 <u>Social (SO)</u>:

*Social instinct* is 3rd in the series of three instinctual variants. Someone having this as their primary instinct receives fuel to their emotion or *rasa* from the broader structure they are part of, whether living or non-living. And these attributes could be positive, negative, or neutral.
