## 1. Abstract

The **Honeycomb Computational Cognition Model (HCC)** is a typological framework for describing differences in the default computation of information by a subject. It distinguishes two attitudes, *Introversion* and *Extroversion*, describing *decompositional* and *compositional* processing, respectively, from four information elements: *Sensing*, *Intuition*, *Thinking*, and *Feeling*. These six primitives combine into functions representing distinct modes of processing particular forms of information. [1]

HCC is constrained by axioms intended to distinguish default computational mode from goals, abilities, prior experience, resources, environmental conditions, and intelligence. Functions may interact through a hierarchical architecture of primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary function sets, allowing alternative computational modes to become accessible when processing encounters obstacles or requires different information. From this architecture, HCC derives sixteen typological configurations represented through its own notation. [2]

The model is intended as a computationally oriented extension within the broader Jungian family of typological theories. It describes patterns of information processing rather than psychological health, mental disorders, or clinical conditions, and is not intended as a diagnostic instrument. [3]



## 2. Basic Terminologies


### 2.1 <u>Subjective Baseline</u>:

- **Consciousness:** Ability to have a default computational mode. [4]
- **Ego:** Ability to differentiate between subject and object. [5]
- **Content:** Information that comes into attention of consciousness. [6]


### 2.2 <u>Sources Of Information</u>:

- **Subject:** A processing function made by combining consciousness and ego. [7]
- **Object:** Everything other the subject. [8]
- **System:** Alternative term used for *object*. [9]


### 2.3 <u>Primitives Types</u>:

- **Information elements ($S$, $N$, $T$, $F$):** A form of information that the system possesses. [10]
- **Attitude ($I$, $E$):** Mode of processing information that the system possesses. [11]



## 3. Constraints On Primitives


### 3.1 <u>List Of Primitives</u>:

- Introversion ($I$) [12]
- Extroversion ($E$) [13]
- Sensing ($S$) [14]
- Intuition ($N$) [15]
- Thinking ($T$) [16]
- Feeling ($F$) [17]


### 3.2 <u>Axioms To Satisfy</u>:

1. Its all about default information computation mode. [18]
2. Its not about end goal, or ability to perform preferred action. [19]
3. It can exist without prior experience, or any kind of resources. [20]
4. It can exist under any form of immediate surrounding or condition. [21]
5. Level of intelligence doesn't affect which mode is there by default. [22]
6. One primitive can not reproduce another in any form. [23]



## 4. Attitude


### 4.1 <u>Introversion (I)</u>:

A mode of processing information in which the subject *decomposes* system with information into sub-systems, or dwells on a certain stage of decomposition graph, keeping the net direction of travelling as *semi-negative*. It can keep *decomposing* each sub-system into smaller sub-systems. [24]


### 4.2 <u>Extroversion (E)</u>:

A mode of processing information in which the subject *composes* larger system using information embedded in systems, or dwells on a certain stage of decomposition graph, keeping the net direction of travelling as *semi-positive*. It can keep *composing* each system into larger system. [25]



## 5. Information Types


### 5.1 <u>Impressionistic Information (S/N)</u>:

A category of information types which is *impressionistic* in nature, dealing with immediate *impression* that a system triggers in mind of the subject. [26]


### 5.2 <u>Evaluative Information (T/F)</u>:

A category of information types which is *evaluative* in nature, dealing with *values* that a system possesses, whether concretely or in abstraction. [27]



## 6. Information Elements


### 6.1 <u>Sensing (S)</u>:

A form of information which contains the *sensory aspects* of the system under consideration of subject. These information are directly perceived by the senses. [28]


### 6.2 <u>Intuition (N)</u>:

A form of information which contains the *latent aspects* of the system under consideration of subject. These information are indirect and generally hidden. [29]


### 6.3 <u>Thinking (T)</u>:

A form of information which contains the *factual aspects* of the system under consideration of subject. These information are based on laws and logic. [30]


### 6.4 <u>Feeling (F)</u>:

A form of information which contains the *affinitive aspects* of the system under consideration of subject. These information are based on personal or group preferences. [31]



## 7. Functions


### 7.1 <u>Introverted Sensing (Si)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *introversion* and information element of *sensing*. This function is used to decompose the *sensory aspects* of a system into *sensory sub-systems* revealing more *sensory information*, and those sub-system can be broken down further for indefinite number of times. [32]

For example, the sensory information a pen presents could be its color, weight, thickness, design, etc. Using *introverted sensing*, one might receive a sensory impression of certain class of plastic container having similar design. And that plastic container can further provide sensory impression of domestic kitchen items, and so on. [33]


### 7.2 <u>Extroverted Sensing (Se)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *extroversion* and information element of *sensing*. This function is used to compose a *larger sensory system* using presented *sensory information* in systems, and those systems could be further composed into even larger system for indefinite number of times. [34]

For example, the sensory information a pen presents could be its color, weight, thickness, design, etc. Using *extroverted sensing*, one might use the sensory information embedded in that pen for his advantage, like in situation where pen can be used as a means of self-defense for its sensory nature, joining it into a combat system where it produces a sensory effect. [35]


### 7.3 <u>Introverted Intuition (Ni)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *introversion* and information element of *intuition*. This function is used to decompose the *latent aspects* of a system into *latent sub-systems* revealing more *latent information*, and those sub-system can be broken down further for indefinite number of times. [36]

For example, the latent information a pen presents could be its symbolic meaning, identity, etc. Using *introverted intuition*, one might receive a latent impression of a religious figure. Then he might further decompose the latent information this figure contains to find its equivalent representation in real world and similar person the subject came across. [37]


### 7.4 <u>Extroverted Intuition (Ne)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *extroversion* and information element of *intuition*. This function is used to compose a *larger latent system* using presented *latent information* in systems, and those systems could be further composed into even larger system for indefinite number of times. [38]

For example, the latent information a pen presents could be its symbolic meaning, identity, etc. Using *extroverted intuition*, one might use the latent information embedded in that pen to join it with other latent systems to collectively form an abstract art containing symbol that pen and similar systems represent. Then he might even form larger work from this, and so on. [39]


### 7.5 <u>Introverted Thinking (Ti)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *introversion* and information element of *thinking*. This function is used to decompose the *factual aspects* of a system into *factual sub-systems* revealing more *factual information*, and those sub-system can be broken down further for indefinite number of times. [40]

For example, the factual information a pen presents could be that it can write with certain color, it costs five bucks, and ends after certain time, etc. Using *introverted thinking*, one might decompose these factual information into more factual sub-systems. Like the pen is able to write because it contains ink, ends because it has refill, and endures pressure for being made from copper. [41]


### 7.6 <u>Extroverted Thinking (Te)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *extroversion* and information element of *thinking*. This function is used to compose a *larger factual system* using presented *factual information* in systems, and those systems could be further composed into even larger system for indefinite number of times. [42]

For example, the factual information a pen presents could be that it can write with certain color, it costs five bucks, and ends after certain time, etc. Using *extroverted thinking*, one might take other systems like paper materials, notebook built, and then combine pen into that factual system to conclude that notebook can safely hold ink from flowing. [43]


### 7.7 <u>Introverted Feeling (Fi)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *introversion* and information element of *feeling*. This function is used to decompose the *affinitive aspects* of a system into *affinitive sub-systems* revealing more *affinitive information*, and those sub-system can be broken down further for indefinite number of times. [44]

For example, the affinitive information a pen presents could be its beauty, social value, positivity, etc. Using *introverted feeling*, one might decompose these affinitive information into smaller affinitive sub-systems, revealing themes like loyalty and honesty. And then further decompose them, and so on. [45]


### 7.8 <u>Extroverted Feeling (Fe)</u>:

Combines the attitude of *extroversion* and information element of *feeling*. This function is used to compose a *larger affinitive system* using presented *affinitive information* in systems, and those systems could be further composed into even larger system for indefinite number of times. [46]

For example, the affinitive information a pen presents could be its beauty, social value, positivity, etc. Using *extroverted feeling*, one might take other affinitive systems like collective values and religious beliefs, to form a larger system displaying atmosphere of heterogeneous values. [47]



## 8. Function Stack


Basic rule is that the stack follows this table, where the default computation mode decreases when moving right and downward: [48]

| $p$  | $s1$ |  -  |
| :--: | :--: | :-: |
| $s2$ | $t$  |  -  |
|  -   |  -   | $q$ |


### 8.1 <u>Primary Function Set (p)</u>:

Default computation mode, where the attention of subject goes majority of time. This set contains one function formed with a dominant attitude and dominant information element. Like dominant $I$ and dominant $T$ forms $Ti$. [49]

For example, a person with $Ti$ primary function might spend majority of the time decomposing facts behind the systems they encounter like electronic systems, theoretical formulae, or even social hierarchy, but processed in terms of facts instead of affinitive values it presents. [50]


### 8.2 <u>Secondary Function Set (s)</u>:

Takes cover when the *primary function* is stuck somewhere in composition or decomposition process. This set contains two functions, one with same information element as primary, but altered attitude. Or same attitude but one of the other information element type. Like $Ti$ will have secondary $\{Te,Ni\}$ or $\{Te,Si\}$. [51]

Continuing previous example, if $Ti$ is stuck decomposing systems, it might try combining decomposed sub-systems in different manner to understand it ($Te$), or decompose its latent information ($Ni$). [52]


### 8.3 <u>Tertiary Function Set (t)</u>:

Controls processing when even *secondary function set* gets stuck while composing or decomposing information. This set contains one function, which is same as the function in *secondary function set* with information element other than the primary one's, but altered attitude. Like $Ti$ will have tertiary $Se$ or $Ne$. [53]

Continuing same example, if $Te$ and $Ni$ are stuck for the subject, it will try combining the latent aspects of the system in a certain way to produce an abstract system ($Ne$). [54]


### 8.4 <u>Quaternary Function Set (q)</u>:

Works as an accessible toolkit when the subject needs to perform activities or processing that requires other functions. This set contains remaining four function. Like $Ti$ with $Ne$ auxiliary will have $\{Si,Se,Fi,Fe\}$. [55]

Continuing same example, a person with *primary function set* of $Ti$ and *tertiary function set* $Ne$ would be able to understand affinitive values and systems by accessing $Fi$ or $Fe$ functions. While also using functions like $Si$ or $Se$ to interact with the physical aspects of world. [56]

### 9.2 <u>Type Formation Rules</u>:

1. The attitude of only function in *primary function set* is written as first letter. For example, primary function $Ti$ would mean notation is $D\_\_$. [63]
2. Then second letter is the information element involved with the *primary function*. For example, primary function $Ti$ would mean notation is now $DL\_$. [64]
3. And finally the third letter is the information element involved with the *tertiary function*. For example, depending on whether its $Se$ or $Ne$, primary function $Ti$ will be represented as $DLS$ or $DLI$. [65]

## 10. Important Notes


- Honeycomb Computation Model is a typological framework, not a mental health diagnostic tool. [82]
- It describes default information-processing modes, not psychological disorders or clinical conditions. [83]
- Its primitives are intended to characterize computation, not symptoms. [84]
- A type classification does not indicate whether a person is mentally healthy or unhealthy. [85]
- No type is inherently pathological, superior, inferior, normal, or abnormal. [86]
- Psychological distress, psychiatric symptoms, or clinical impairment cannot be inferred from a person's Honeycomb type. [87]
- The model does not replace clinical assessment, diagnosis, or treatment by qualified mental health professionals. [88]
- Behavioral similarities to a particular type should not be interpreted as evidence of a mental disorder. [89]
- Environmental stress, illness, trauma, medication, or other clinical factors should not be treated as typological evidence. [90]
- The model concerns *"how information is processed by default", not "whether the person is psychologically well"*. [91]
