# Chapter 1: Basics of Economics

> **Purpose:** Understand the fundamental concepts of economics, economic systems, goods, factors of production, demand, supply and markets.

> **Exam Tip:** AFCAT generally asks **basic definitions and comparisons**, not mathematical derivations.

---

# Background

**Economics** is the study of:

> **How people and societies use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants.**

Central Problem:

**Scarcity**

Because:

- Resources are limited.
- Human wants are unlimited.

---

# Basic Economic Problem

Every economy must answer three questions:

1. **What to produce?**
2. **How to produce?**
3. **For whom to produce?**

---

# Economy

An **economy** is the system through which goods and services are:

- Produced
- Distributed
- Consumed

---

# Types of Economy

## Capitalist Economy

Characteristics:

- Private ownership.
- Profit motive.
- Market decides prices.

Examples:

- USA
- Singapore

---

## Socialist Economy

Characteristics:

- Government ownership.
- Central planning.
- Social welfare.

Example:

- Cuba

---

## Mixed Economy

Characteristics:

- Public + Private sectors.
- Market + Government.

Example:

**India**

---

# Microeconomics

Studies:

Individual economic units.

Examples:

- Consumer
- Firm
- Household

Focus:

- Price determination.
- Consumer behaviour.

---

# Macroeconomics

Studies:

Entire economy.

Focus:

- GDP
- Inflation
- Unemployment
- National Income

---

# Micro vs Macro

| Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
|----------------|----------------|
| Individual Units | Entire Economy |
| Prices | GDP |
| Consumer | Inflation |
| Firm | Unemployment |

---

# Human Wants

Characteristics:

- Unlimited.
- Recurring.
- Competitive.
- Can be satisfied only partially.

---

# Goods

Goods are tangible items that satisfy human wants.

---

## Types of Goods

### Free Goods

Available naturally.

Examples:

- Air
- Sunlight

---

### Economic Goods

Limited in supply.

Examples:

- Food
- Car
- Mobile Phone

---

### Consumer Goods

Used directly by consumers.

Example:

- Clothes

---

### Capital Goods

Used to produce other goods.

Examples:

- Machines
- Factory Equipment

---

### Durable Goods

Used repeatedly.

Examples:

- Refrigerator
- Car

---

### Perishable Goods

Consumed quickly.

Examples:

- Milk
- Fruits

---

# Services

Services are:

**Intangible economic activities**.

Examples:

- Banking
- Education
- Healthcare
- Transport

---

# Utility

Meaning:

Ability of a good or service to satisfy human wants.

> **Utility does not necessarily mean usefulness.**
A product may have utility simply because someone wants it.

---

# Wealth

Anything that:

- Has value.
- Is scarce.
- Can be owned.
- Has utility.

---

# Factors of Production

Four Factors:

| Factor | Reward |
|----------|--------|
| Land | Rent |
| Labour | Wages |
| Capital | Interest |
| Entrepreneurship | Profit |

---

## Land

Includes:

- Natural Resources
- Water
- Minerals
- Forests

Reward:

**Rent**

---

## Labour

Human effort.

Reward:

**Wages**

---

## Capital

Man-made resources used in production.

Examples:

- Machinery
- Buildings
- Tools

Reward:

**Interest**

---

## Entrepreneurship

Organizes all factors.

Takes:

Risk

Reward:

**Profit**

---

# Opportunity Cost

Meaning:

Value of the **next best alternative** that is given up.

Example:

Choosing to study for AFCAT instead of watching a movie.

Opportunity Cost:

Movie missed.

---

# Production

Production means:

Creation of utility.

Not merely manufacturing.

Examples:

- Farming
- Banking
- Teaching

---

# Consumption

Using goods and services to satisfy wants.

---

# Demand

Demand means:

**Desire + Ability + Willingness to Pay**

---

## Law of Demand

When:

Price increases

↓

Demand decreases

When:

Price decreases

↓

Demand increases

Provided:

**Other factors remain constant (Ceteris Paribus).**

---

# Supply

Supply means:

Quantity producers are willing to sell.

---

## Law of Supply

When:

Price increases

↓

Supply increases

When:

Price decreases

↓

Supply decreases

---

# Market

A market is:

A place or system where buyers and sellers interact.

Examples:

- Local Market
- Stock Market
- Online Marketplace

---

# Types of Market

## Perfect Competition

- Many buyers.
- Many sellers.
- Identical products.

---

## Monopoly

Only one seller.

Example:

Government monopoly in certain sectors (historically).

---

## Oligopoly

Few large sellers.

Example:

Telecom industry (typical example).

---

## Monopolistic Competition

Many sellers with differentiated products.

Example:

Restaurants.

---

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Goods ≠ Services

Goods:
- Tangible

Services:
- Intangible

---

### Capital ≠ Money

Capital:
- Productive assets (machines, tools, buildings).

Money:
- Medium of exchange.

---

### Demand ≠ Desire

Desire:
- Wish only.

Demand:
- Desire + Ability + Willingness to Pay.

---

### Production ≠ Manufacturing

Manufacturing:
- Producing physical goods.

Production:
- Creating utility (includes goods and services).

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ Economics is the study of money only.

✅ Economics studies **how limited resources are used to satisfy unlimited wants**.

---

❌ Capital means cash or currency.

✅ In economics, **capital** refers to **man-made productive assets** such as machines, factories and tools.

---

❌ Demand means simply wanting a product.

✅ Demand requires:
- **Desire**
- **Ability to pay**
- **Willingness to pay**

---

❌ Production only means manufacturing goods.

✅ Production means **creating utility**, including both **goods and services**.

---

❌ India follows a purely capitalist economy.

✅ India follows a **Mixed Economy**, where both the **public and private sectors** play important roles.
