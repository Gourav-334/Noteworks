# Background

Unlike **Monetary Policy**, which is managed by the **RBI**, **Fiscal Policy** is managed by the **Government of India** through:

- Taxation
- Government Expenditure
- Borrowing

Main Objective:

- Economic Growth
- Employment
- Price Stability
- Income Distribution

---

# Fiscal Policy

Meaning:

Government policy relating to:

- Taxes
- Public Expenditure
- Borrowing

Prepared by:

**Ministry of Finance**

---

# Union Budget

Meaning:

Annual financial statement of the Government of India.

Constitutional Provision:

**Article 112**

Presented by:

**Finance Minister**

Presented in:

**Lok Sabha**

---

# Budget Components

Two Main Parts:

- Revenue Budget
- Capital Budget

---

# Revenue Budget

Deals with:

Recurring income and expenditure.

Includes:

Revenue Receipts

and

Revenue Expenditure.

---

# Capital Budget

Deals with:

- Asset Creation
- Investments
- Borrowing
- Loans

---

# Revenue Receipts

Government income that does **not create liabilities**.

Examples:

- Taxes
- Dividends
- Fees
- Interest Receipts

---

# Capital Receipts

Government income that:

Creates liabilities

or

Reduces assets.

Examples:

- Borrowing
- Loan Recovery
- Disinvestment

---

# Revenue Expenditure

Government spending that:

Does **not create assets**.

Examples:

- Salaries
- Pensions
- Subsidies
- Interest Payments

---

# Capital Expenditure

Government spending that:

Creates assets

or

Reduces liabilities.

Examples:

- Roads
- Railways
- Defence Equipment
- Dams
- Infrastructure

---

# Types of Taxes

## Direct Tax

Paid directly to the Government.

Cannot be shifted to another person.

Examples:

- Income Tax
- Corporate Tax

---

## Indirect Tax

Collected through sellers.

Burden can be shifted to consumers.

Examples:

- GST
- Customs Duty

---

# Direct Tax vs Indirect Tax

| Direct Tax | Indirect Tax |
|-------------|--------------|
| Paid Directly | Paid Indirectly |
| Cannot be Shifted | Burden can be Shifted |
| Income Tax | GST |

---

# Goods and Services Tax (GST)

Implemented:

**1 July 2017**

Type:

Indirect Tax.

Replaced:

Many Central and State indirect taxes.

Objective:

**One Nation, One Tax**

---

# Types of GST

## CGST

Central Goods and Services Tax.

Collected by:

Central Government.

---

## SGST

State Goods and Services Tax.

Collected by:

State Government.

---

## IGST

Integrated Goods and Services Tax.

Applicable to:

Inter-State Trade.

Collected by:

Central Government.

---

## UTGST

Union Territory Goods and Services Tax.

Applicable in:

Union Territories without legislature.

---

# GST Council

Constitutional Provision:

**Article 279A**

Chairperson:

**Union Finance Minister**

Functions:

- Recommend GST rates.
- Recommend exemptions.
- Improve GST implementation.

---

# Public Debt

Meaning:

Money borrowed by the Government.

Sources:

- Internal Borrowing
- External Borrowing

---

# Deficits

A deficit occurs when:

Government expenditure exceeds income.

---

# Revenue Deficit

Formula:

Revenue Expenditure

−

Revenue Receipts

Meaning:

Government cannot meet day-to-day expenses through normal income.

---

# Fiscal Deficit

Meaning:

Total expenditure exceeds:

Total receipts

(excluding borrowings).

Indicates:

Government borrowing requirement.

> **Most important deficit for AFCAT.**

---

# Primary Deficit

Formula:

Fiscal Deficit

−

Interest Payments

---

# Fiscal Deficit vs Revenue Deficit

| Fiscal Deficit | Revenue Deficit |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Overall Borrowing Need | Revenue Shortfall |
| Includes Capital Spending | Revenue Spending Only |

---

# Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act

Enacted:

**2003**

Purpose:

- Reduce Fiscal Deficit.
- Maintain Fiscal Discipline.
- Improve Budget Transparency.

---

# Subsidy

Meaning:

Financial assistance given by the Government.

Examples:

- Fertilizer Subsidy
- Food Subsidy
- LPG Subsidy

---

# Disinvestment

Meaning:

Government sells shares in Public Sector Enterprises.

Purpose:

- Raise Revenue.
- Improve Efficiency.

---

# Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs)

Companies where:

Government owns majority stake.

Examples:

- ONGC
- BHEL
- SAIL

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Fiscal Policy ≠ Monetary Policy

Fiscal:
- Government

Monetary:
- RBI

---

### Revenue Budget ≠ Capital Budget

Revenue:
- Day-to-day Income & Expenditure

Capital:
- Assets & Borrowing

---

### Revenue Expenditure ≠ Capital Expenditure

Revenue:
- No Asset Creation

Capital:
- Asset Creation

---

### Revenue Deficit ≠ Fiscal Deficit

Revenue Deficit:
- Revenue Shortfall

Fiscal Deficit:
- Total Borrowing Need

---

### Direct Tax ≠ Indirect Tax

Direct:
- Paid by Same Person

Indirect:
- Burden Shifted

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ The RBI presents the Union Budget.

✅ The **Union Budget** is presented by the **Finance Minister** in the **Lok Sabha**.

---

❌ GST is a Direct Tax.

✅ **GST** is an **Indirect Tax** because its burden is generally passed on to consumers.

---

❌ Fiscal Policy is managed by the RBI.

✅ **Fiscal Policy** is managed by the **Government of India**.
The **RBI** manages **Monetary Policy**.

---

❌ Revenue Expenditure creates productive assets.

✅ **Revenue Expenditure** covers recurring expenses such as salaries, pensions and subsidies.
**Capital Expenditure** creates assets.

---

❌ Fiscal Deficit and Revenue Deficit mean the same thing.

✅ **Revenue Deficit** concerns the gap in the **Revenue Budget**.
**Fiscal Deficit** measures the Government's **overall borrowing requirement**.
