# Chapter 3: Longitudes & Latitudes

> **Purpose:** Understand Earth's imaginary lines used for locating places, measuring distance, and calculating time.

> **Exam Tip:** AFCAT frequently asks about the **Equator, Tropics, Polar Circles, Prime Meridian, International Date Line (IDL), Indian Standard Time (IST)** and basic time calculations.

---

# Background

The Earth is divided by imaginary lines into:

- **Latitudes** (East–West circles)
- **Longitudes** (North–South semicircles)

Together, they form a **Geographical Grid** used to locate any place on Earth.

---

# Latitudes

Latitudes are:

Imaginary circles drawn parallel to the Equator.

Measure:

Distance **North or South** of the Equator.

Unit:

Degrees (°)

Range:

**0° to 90° N** and **0° to 90° S**

---

# Characteristics of Latitudes

- Run **East–West**.
- Never meet each other.
- Parallel to one another.
- Used to determine **climate**.

---

# Important Latitudes

| Latitude | Position | Importance |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| 90° N | North Pole | Northern end of Earth's axis |
| 66½° N | Arctic Circle | Polar Day & Polar Night |
| 23½° N | Tropic of Cancer | Sun overhead on 21 June |
| 0° | Equator | Longest Latitude |
| 23½° S | Tropic of Capricorn | Sun overhead on 22 December |
| 66½° S | Antarctic Circle | Polar Day & Polar Night |
| 90° S | South Pole | Southern end of Earth's axis |

---

# Equator

Latitude:

**0°**

Characteristics:

- Longest latitude.
- Divides Earth into:
  - Northern Hemisphere
  - Southern Hemisphere

Receives:

Maximum sunlight throughout the year.

---

# Tropic of Cancer

Latitude:

**23½° N**

Sun is directly overhead:

Around **21 June**

Passes through **8 Indian States**.

# Arctic Circle

Latitude:

**66½° N**

Known for:

- Midnight Sun
- Polar Night

---

# Antarctic Circle

Latitude:

**66½° S**

Known for:

- Continuous daylight in summer.
- Continuous darkness in winter.

---

# Longitudes

Longitudes are:

Imaginary semicircles joining:

- North Pole
- South Pole

Measure:

Distance **East or West** of the Prime Meridian.

Range:

**0° to 180° East** and **0° to 180° West**

---

# Characteristics of Longitudes

- Run **North–South**.
- Meet at both poles.
- Used for calculating **time**.

---

# Prime Meridian

Longitude:

**0°**

Passes through:

**Greenwich, London (United Kingdom)**

Known as:

**Greenwich Meridian**

Time based on it:

**Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)**

Now internationally standardized as:

**UTC (Coordinated Universal Time)**

---

# International Date Line (IDL)

Longitude:

Approximately **180°**

Purpose:

Date changes when crossing it.

Rule:

- Cross **West → East** → **Subtract one day**
- Cross **East → West** → **Add one day**

> **The IDL is not perfectly straight**; it zigzags to avoid dividing countries and island groups.

---

# Longitude & Time

Earth rotates:

**360° in 24 hours**

Therefore:

- **15° = 1 hour**
- **1° = 4 minutes**

---

# Local Time

Places on different longitudes have different local times.

Example:

15° difference

↓

1-hour time difference.

---

# Standard Time

A country adopts one standard longitude for official time.

Purpose:

Avoid confusion caused by multiple local times.

---

# Indian Standard Time (IST)

Standard Meridian:

**82½° E**

Passes near:

**Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh**

Time:

**UTC +5:30**

> India uses a **single time zone** despite its wide east-west extent.

---

# Indian Extent

| Direction | Approximate Longitude |
|------------|----------------------:|
| Westernmost | 68°7′ E |
| Easternmost | 97°25′ E |

Time difference between extreme ends:

Approximately **2 hours**.

Yet the entire country follows:

**IST (UTC +5:30)**

---

# Great Circles

A **Great Circle** is the largest possible circle on a sphere.

Examples:

- Equator
- Any circle formed by opposite meridians (180° apart)

Importance:

- Shortest route between two places.
- Used in air and sea navigation.

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Latitude ≠ Longitude

Latitude:
- East–West
- Parallel
- Climate

Longitude:
- North–South
- Meet at Poles
- Time

---

### Equator ≠ Prime Meridian

Equator:
- Latitude
- Divides North & South

Prime Meridian:
- Longitude
- Divides East & West

---

### GMT ≠ IST

GMT/UTC:
- 0°

IST:
- 82½° E
- UTC +5:30

---

### Tropic of Cancer ≠ Tropic of Capricorn

Cancer:
- 23½° N
- June Solstice

Capricorn:
- 23½° S
- December Solstice

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ The Equator and Prime Meridian are the same.

✅ The **Equator** is **0° Latitude**.
The **Prime Meridian** is **0° Longitude**.

---

❌ IST is based on 90° E Longitude.

✅ IST is based on **82½° E Longitude**, passing near **Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh)**.

---

❌ Crossing the International Date Line always adds one day.

✅ Crossing **East → West** adds one day.
Crossing **West → East** subtracts one day.

---

❌ The Tropic of Cancer passes through 7 Indian States.

✅ It passes through **8 Indian States**.

---

❌ All latitudes are Great Circles.

✅ Only the **Equator** is a **Great Circle** among the latitudes.
Other latitudes are **Small Circles**.
