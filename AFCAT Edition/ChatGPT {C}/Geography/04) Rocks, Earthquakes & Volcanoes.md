# Background

The Earth's interior consists of three major layers:

- Crust
- Mantle
- Core

Most earthquakes and volcanoes occur due to the movement of **tectonic plates**.

---

# Interior of the Earth

| Layer | Approx. Depth | Characteristics |
|--------|--------------:|-----------------|
| Crust | 5–70 km | Thin, solid outer layer |
| Mantle | ~2,900 km | Semi-solid, convection currents |
| Core | ~3,480 km | Mainly Iron & Nickel (NiFe) |

---

## Crust

Two Types:

### Continental Crust

- Thicker
- Granite (SiAl)

### Oceanic Crust

- Thinner
- Basalt (SiMa)

---

## Mantle

Largest layer.

Contains:

- Semi-molten rocks.
- Convection currents.

Importance:

Movement of tectonic plates.

---

## Core

Composition:

- Iron
- Nickel

Two Parts:

### Outer Core

- Liquid

### Inner Core

- Solid

Reason:

Extremely high pressure.

---

# Plate Tectonics

The Earth's crust is divided into:

**Tectonic Plates**

These plates constantly move.

Types of Plate Boundaries:

- Convergent
- Divergent
- Transform

---

## Convergent Boundary

Plates move:

Towards each other.

Produces:

- Fold Mountains
- Deep Ocean Trenches
- Strong Earthquakes
- Volcanoes

Example:

**Himalayas**

---

## Divergent Boundary

Plates move:

Away from each other.

Produces:

- Mid-Ocean Ridges
- Rift Valleys
- New Crust

---

## Transform Boundary

Plates slide past each other.

Produces:

- Earthquakes

Example:

**San Andreas Fault (USA)**

---

# Rocks

Rocks are naturally occurring solid masses of minerals.

Three Main Types:

- Igneous
- Sedimentary
- Metamorphic

---

# Igneous Rocks

Formed by:

Cooling of magma or lava.

Known as:

**Primary Rocks**

Characteristics:

- Hard
- Crystalline
- No fossils

Examples:

- Granite
- Basalt

---

# Sedimentary Rocks

Formed by:

Deposition and compaction of sediments.

Characteristics:

- Layered
- Soft
- Fossils commonly found

Examples:

- Sandstone
- Limestone
- Shale

---

# Metamorphic Rocks

Formed by:

Transformation of existing rocks due to:

- Heat
- Pressure

Examples:

- Marble (from Limestone)
- Slate (from Shale)
- Quartzite (from Sandstone)
- Gneiss (from Granite)

---

# Rock Cycle

```text
Magma
 ↓
Igneous Rock
 ↓ (Weathering & Deposition)
Sedimentary Rock
 ↓ (Heat & Pressure)
Metamorphic Rock
 ↓ (Melting)
Magma
```

---

# Earthquake

An earthquake is the sudden shaking of the Earth's surface due to:

- Movement of tectonic plates.
- Release of stored energy.

---

# Focus & Epicentre

## Focus (Hypocentre)

Point inside the Earth where the earthquake originates.

---

## Epicentre

Point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus.

Maximum damage usually occurs near the epicentre.

---

# Seismic Waves

Three Main Types:

| Wave | Travels Through | Speed |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| P-Waves | Solids, Liquids & Gases | Fastest |
| S-Waves | Solids only | Slower |
| Surface Waves | Earth's Surface | Most destructive |

---

# Measurement of Earthquakes

## Magnitude

Measured by:

**Richter Scale** *(historically)*

Modern Instrument:

**Moment Magnitude Scale (Mw)**

---

## Intensity

Measured by:

**Modified Mercalli Intensity (MMI) Scale**

Measures:

Observed damage and effects.

---

India is divided into:

- Zone II
- Zone III
- Zone IV
- Zone V

Most earthquake-prone:

**Zone V**

Includes parts of:

- Kashmir
- Northeast India
- Andaman & Nicobar Islands
- Rann of Kutch

---

# Volcano

A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust through which:

- Lava
- Ash
- Gases

escape.

---

# Parts of a Volcano

- Magma Chamber
- Vent
- Crater
- Lava Flow
- Ash Cloud

---

# Types of Volcanoes

## Active

Erupts frequently.

Example:

- Mount Etna (Italy)

---

## Dormant

Currently inactive but may erupt again.

Example:

- Mount Fuji (Japan)

---

## Extinct

No expected future eruption.

Example:

- Mount Kenya

---

# Lava vs Magma

### Magma

Molten rock **below** the Earth's surface.

### Lava

Molten rock **after reaching** the surface.

---

# Ring of Fire

Location:

Around the **Pacific Ocean**

Characteristics:

- Most active volcanoes.
- Frequent earthquakes.

Contains about:

**75% of the world's active volcanoes.**

# Tsunami

Caused mainly by:

- Undersea earthquakes.
- Volcanic eruptions.
- Underwater landslides.

> **Not every earthquake causes a tsunami—only significant undersea disturbances do.**

- Solids, Liquids & Gases

- Solids only

- Cooling
- No fossils

- Deposition
- Fossils

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ Granite is a sedimentary rock.

✅ **Granite is an igneous rock.**

---

❌ S-Waves travel through liquids.

✅ **S-Waves travel only through solids.**
**P-Waves** travel through solids, liquids and gases.

---

❌ The epicentre is inside the Earth.

✅ The **focus** is inside the Earth.
The **epicentre** is directly above it on the Earth's surface.

---

❌ India has several active volcanoes.

✅ **Barren Island** is the **only active volcano in India**.

---

❌ All earthquakes generate tsunamis.

✅ Only **major undersea earthquakes or similar disturbances** can generate a tsunami.
