# Chapter 7: Cyclones & Anticyclones

> **Purpose:** Understand the formation, characteristics and types of cyclones and anticyclones.

> **Exam Tip:** AFCAT frequently asks about **Cyclones vs Anticyclones, Tropical vs Temperate Cyclones, Coriolis Force, eye of a cyclone, and cyclone naming**.

---

# Background

Cyclones and anticyclones are large-scale atmospheric systems formed due to:

- Pressure differences
- Earth's rotation (Coriolis Force)
- Temperature variations

---

# Cyclone

A cyclone is a:

**Low-pressure system**

Air moves:

**Towards the centre**

Air movement:

**Spirals upward**

Associated Weather:

- Heavy rainfall
- Strong winds
- Thunderstorms

---

# Formation of Cyclones

Requirements:

- Warm ocean water (above **26.5°C**)
- Moist air
- Low-pressure area
- Coriolis Force
- Low vertical wind shear

---

# Structure of a Tropical Cyclone

Main Parts:

- Eye
- Eyewall
- Rain Bands

---

## Eye

Characteristics:

- Centre of cyclone.
- Calm weather.
- Lowest pressure.
- Light winds.

---

## Eyewall

Surrounds the eye.

Characteristics:

- Strongest winds.
- Heaviest rainfall.
- Most destructive region.

---

## Rain Bands

Outer spiral bands.

Produce:

- Heavy rain.
- Thunderstorms.

---

# Rotation of Cyclones

### Northern Hemisphere

Rotate:

**Anti-clockwise**

---

### Southern Hemisphere

Rotate:

**Clockwise**

Reason:

**Coriolis Force**

---

# Types of Cyclones

## Tropical Cyclones

Form over:

Warm tropical oceans.

Characteristics:

- Warm core.
- Very strong winds.
- Heavy rainfall.

Diameter:

Approximately **100–1000 km**

---

## Temperate (Extratropical) Cyclones

Form in:

Mid-latitudes.

Characteristics:

- Cold core.
- Form where warm and cold air masses meet (fronts).
- Larger than tropical cyclones.

---

# Tropical Cyclone vs Temperate Cyclone

| Tropical Cyclone | Temperate Cyclone |
|------------------|-------------------|
| Warm Core | Cold Core |
| No Fronts | Associated with Fronts |
| Tropical Oceans | Mid-Latitudes |
| More Symmetrical | Less Symmetrical |
| Eye Present | Eye usually absent |

---

# Cyclone Naming

Cyclones are named to:

- Improve communication.
- Avoid confusion.
- Assist disaster management.

In the North Indian Ocean, names are assigned by:

**World Meteorological Organization (WMO)** using lists contributed by member countries.

---

# Cyclones in India

Most Affected Seas:

- Bay of Bengal
- Arabian Sea

---

## Bay of Bengal

Produces:

More cyclones.

Reasons:

- Warmer water.
- Larger basin.
- Higher humidity.
- Favorable atmospheric conditions.

---

## Cyclone Seasons in India

Main Seasons:

- April–June (Pre-Monsoon)
- October–December (Post-Monsoon)

# Anticyclone

An anticyclone is a:

**High-pressure system**

Air moves:

**Away from the centre**

Air movement:

**Sinks downward**

Associated Weather:

- Clear skies.
- Dry weather.
- Stable atmosphere.

---

# Rotation of Anticyclones

### Northern Hemisphere

Rotate:

**Clockwise**

---

### Southern Hemisphere

Rotate:

**Anti-clockwise**

---

# Cyclone vs Anticyclone

| Cyclone | Anticyclone |
|----------|-------------|
| Low Pressure | High Pressure |
| Air Converges | Air Diverges |
| Air Rises | Air Sinks |
| Cloudy | Clear Sky |
| Heavy Rain | Dry Weather |

---

# Coriolis Force

Caused by:

Earth's Rotation.

Effects:

- Deflects moving air.
- Deflects ocean currents.

Direction:

### Northern Hemisphere

Deflects to the:

**Right**

### Southern Hemisphere

Deflects to the:

**Left**

---

# Storm Surge

Storm Surge:

Abnormal rise of sea level caused by:

- Cyclone winds.
- Low atmospheric pressure.

Responsible for:

Most cyclone-related coastal deaths.

---

# Tornado vs Cyclone

| Tornado | Cyclone |
|----------|----------|
| Small | Large |
| Land | Mostly Ocean |
| Very Short Duration | Several Days |
| Extremely High Wind Speed | Lower than Tornado |

---

# Hurricane, Typhoon & Cyclone

These are **the same weather phenomenon**, but with different regional names.

| Region | Name |
|---------|------|
| Atlantic & Eastern Pacific | Hurricane |
| Western Pacific | Typhoon |
| Indian Ocean & South Pacific | Cyclone |

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Cyclone ≠ Anticyclone

Cyclone:
- Low Pressure
- Air Rises

Anticyclone:
- High Pressure
- Air Sinks

---

### Tropical ≠ Temperate Cyclone

Tropical:
- Warm Core
- No Fronts

Temperate:
- Cold Core
- Fronts Present

---

### Hurricane ≠ Typhoon ≠ Cyclone

All are:

**The same type of storm.**

Difference:

**Region only.**

---

### Eye ≠ Eyewall

Eye:
- Calm
- Lowest Pressure

Eyewall:
- Strongest Winds
- Most Damage

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ The eye of a cyclone is the most destructive part.

✅ The **eye is calm**.
The **eyewall** is the **most destructive** region.

---

❌ Cyclones rotate clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere.

✅ In the **Northern Hemisphere**, cyclones rotate **anti-clockwise**.
Anticyclones rotate **clockwise**.

---

❌ Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea produce the same number of cyclones.

✅ The **Bay of Bengal** produces **significantly more cyclones**.

---

❌ Hurricanes and Typhoons are different weather systems.

✅ They are **the same type of tropical cyclone**, with different names based on the ocean basin.

---

❌ Storm surge is caused only by heavy rainfall.

✅ A **storm surge** is primarily caused by **strong cyclone winds and low atmospheric pressure**, leading to an abnormal rise in sea level.
