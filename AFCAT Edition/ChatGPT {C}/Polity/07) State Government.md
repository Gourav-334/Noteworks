# Governor

## Constitutional Position

- Constitutional Head of the State.
- Nominal Executive.
- Appointed by the President.

> **Real executive power lies with the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers.**

---

## Articles

| Article | Subject |

| 156 | Term |

| 161 | Pardoning Power |
| 163 | Council of Ministers |

---

## Appointment

Appointed by:

**President of India**

---

## Qualifications

- Citizen of India
- Minimum age: **35 years**

---

## Term

- Five years.
- Holds office during the pleasure of the President.

---

## Oath

Administered by:

**Chief Justice of the High Court**

(In his/her absence, the senior-most judge.)

---

# Powers of the Governor

## Executive Powers

- Appoints the Chief Minister.
- Appoints other Ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- Appoints the Advocate General.
- Appoints the State Election Commissioner *(for State Election Commission, subject to constitutional provisions).*

---

## Legislative Powers

- Summons and prorogues the State Legislature.
- Dissolves the Legislative Assembly.
- Gives assent to Bills.
- Can reserve certain Bills for the President's consideration.
- Can promulgate Ordinances.

---

## Financial Powers

- State Budget introduced with Governor's recommendation.
- Money Bills require prior recommendation of the Governor.

---

## Judicial Powers

Article:

**161**

Can grant:

- Pardon*
- Reprieve
- Respite
- Remission
- Commutation

> *The Governor's pardoning power does **not** extend to court-martial cases or death sentences in the same manner as the President's power under Article 72.*

---

# Ordinance

Article:

**213**

Governor may issue an Ordinance when:

- State Legislature is not in session.

---

# Chief Minister

Article:

**164**

Position:

- Real Executive of the State.
- Head of the State Government.

Appointed by:

Governor

Usually:

Leader of the majority party in the Legislative Assembly.

---

# Powers of the Chief Minister

- Heads the Council of Ministers.
- Advises the Governor.
- Allocates portfolios.
- Coordinates administration.
- Chief policy-maker of the State.

---

# Council of Ministers

Articles:

163–164

Purpose:

Aid and advise the Governor.

Categories:

- Cabinet Ministers
- Ministers of State
- Deputy Ministers

---

# Advocate General

Article:

**165**

Highest Law Officer of the State.

Appointed by:

Governor

Can participate in the proceedings of the State Legislature but **cannot vote**.

---

# State Legislature

Article:

**168**

States may have:

- Unicameral Legislature
or
- Bicameral Legislature

---

# Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)

Lower House.

Members:

Directly elected.

Normal Term:

**5 Years**

Can be dissolved.

Money Bills:

Can be introduced **only here**.

---

# Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)

Upper House.

Permanent House.

One-third members retire every **2 years**.

Maximum strength:

Not more than **one-third** of the Legislative Assembly.

Minimum strength:

**40 Members**

> **Not every State has a Legislative Council.**

---

# States with Legislative Councils *(as of current constitutional position)*

- Andhra Pradesh
- Bihar
- Karnataka
- Maharashtra
- Telangana
- Uttar Pradesh

---

# Speaker

Presiding Officer of:

**Legislative Assembly**

---

# Chairman

Presiding Officer of:

**Legislative Council**

---

# Money Bill in the State

Can be introduced:

Only in:

**Legislative Assembly**

Requires:

Governor's recommendation.

Legislative Council:

Can recommend changes only.

Must return within:

**14 days**

---

# High Court

Articles:

214–231

Each State has:

A High Court.

Highest judicial authority in the State.

---

# Writ Jurisdiction

Article:

**226**

High Courts can issue:

- Habeas Corpus
- Mandamus
- Prohibition
- Certiorari
- Quo Warranto

> High Courts have **wider writ jurisdiction** than the Supreme Court because they can issue writs not only for Fundamental Rights but also for other legal rights.

# Important Supreme Court Case

## S. R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

Held:

- President's Rule under Article 356 is subject to judicial review.
- Reinforced the federal structure of the Constitution.

> **AFCAT takeaway:** Article 356 cannot be used arbitrarily.

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Governor ≠ Chief Minister

Governor:
- Constitutional Head

Chief Minister:
- Real Executive

---

### Advocate General ≠ Attorney General

Advocate General:
- State
- Article 165

Attorney General:
- Union
- Article 76

---

### High Court ≠ Supreme Court

High Court:
- Article 226
- Wider writ jurisdiction

Supreme Court:
- Article 32
- Fundamental Rights enforcement

---

### Legislative Council ≠ Rajya Sabha

Legislative Council:
- State Legislature

Rajya Sabha:
- Parliament

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ The Governor is elected by the people.

✅ The **Governor is appointed by the President**.

---

❌ The Chief Minister is the constitutional head of the State.

✅ The **Governor** is the constitutional head.
The **Chief Minister** is the **real executive**.

---

❌ Every State has a Legislative Council.

✅ **Only some States** have a Legislative Council.

---

❌ A Money Bill can originate in the Legislative Council.

✅ It can be introduced **only in the Legislative Assembly**.

---

❌ The Advocate General is the highest law officer of India.

✅ The **Advocate General** is the highest law officer of a **State**.
The **Attorney General** is the highest law officer of the **Union**.
