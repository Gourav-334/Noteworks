# Chapter 3: Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)

> **Part:** III
>
> **Articles:** 12–35
>
> **Known As:** Magna Carta of India
>
> **Purpose:** Protect individual liberty against arbitrary action by the State.

---

# Timeline

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 1950 | Fundamental Rights came into force |
| 1978 | Right to Property removed as a Fundamental Right (44th Amendment) |
| 2002 | Right to Education inserted (86th Amendment) |

---

# What are Fundamental Rights?

- Guaranteed by the Constitution.
- Enforceable by courts.
- Primarily protect citizens from arbitrary State action.
- Can be suspended only under certain constitutional provisions.

---

# Article 12 – Definition of State

"State" includes:

- Central Government
- State Governments
- Parliament
- State Legislatures
- Local Authorities
- Authorities under Government control

---

# Article 13

Provides that:

- Laws violating Fundamental Rights are void to the extent of inconsistency.

---

# Six Fundamental Rights

| Right | Articles |
|--------|----------|
| Right to Equality | 14–18 |
| Right to Freedom | 19–22 |
| Right against Exploitation | 23–24 |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | 25–28 |
| Cultural & Educational Rights | 29–30 |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | 32 |

> **Originally there were seven Fundamental Rights.**
>
> **Right to Property (Article 31)** ceased to be a Fundamental Right after the **44th Constitutional Amendment (1978).**

---

# 1. Right to Equality (Articles 14–18)

---

## Article 14

Equality before Law

Equal Protection of Laws

Borrowed from:

- UK (Equality before Law)
- USA (Equal Protection of Laws)

---

## Article 15

Prohibits discrimination based on:

- Religion
- Race
- Caste
- Sex
- Place of Birth

Allows special provisions for:

- Women
- Children
- Socially and Educationally Backward Classes
- SCs & STs
- EWS (through constitutional amendment)

---

## Article 16

Equality of opportunity in public employment.

Reservation permitted under constitutional provisions.

---

## Article 17

Abolition of Untouchability.

Punishable by law.

---

## Article 18

Abolition of Titles.

Exceptions:

- Military decorations
- Academic distinctions

Examples:

- Param Vir Chakra
- Bharat Ratna *(not considered a hereditary title)*

---

# 2. Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22)

---

## Article 19

Available **only to citizens**.

Six Freedoms:

- Speech & Expression
- Peaceful Assembly
- Form Associations
- Move Freely
- Reside Anywhere
- Practice Any Profession

> **The Right to Property is NO LONGER one of the freedoms under Article 19.**

---

## Article 20

Protection regarding criminal convictions.

Provides protection against:

- Ex post facto laws
- Double jeopardy
- Self-incrimination

---

## Article 21

Right to Life and Personal Liberty.

One of the **most widely interpreted Articles**.

Includes through judicial interpretation:

- Right to Privacy
- Right to Clean Environment
- Right to Livelihood
- Right to Dignity

---

## Article 21A

Right to Education.

Added by:

**86th Constitutional Amendment (2002)**

Provides:

- Free & compulsory education
- Children aged **6–14 years**

---

## Article 22

Protection against arbitrary arrest and detention.

Provides:

- Right to know grounds of arrest
- Right to consult a lawyer
- Produced before magistrate within 24 hours

Contains provisions regarding:

- Preventive Detention

---

# 3. Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24)

---

## Article 23

Prohibits:

- Human trafficking
- Forced labour (Begar)

---

## Article 24

Prohibits employment of children below **14 years** in:

- Factories
- Mines
- Hazardous occupations

---

# 4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25–28)

---

## Article 25

Freedom of conscience.

Freedom to:

- Profess
- Practice
- Propagate religion

Subject to:

- Public Order
- Morality
- Health

---

## Article 26

Freedom to manage religious affairs.

---

## Article 27

No person can be compelled to pay taxes for promoting a particular religion.

---

## Article 28

Freedom regarding religious instruction in educational institutions.

---

# 5. Cultural & Educational Rights (Articles 29–30)

---

## Article 29

Protection of language, script and culture.

---

## Article 30

Minorities have the right to establish and administer educational institutions.

---

# 6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32)

Called by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar:

> **"Heart and Soul of the Constitution."**

Provides citizens the right to directly approach the **Supreme Court** for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

---

# Writs

| Writ | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Habeas Corpus | Produce detained person before court |
| Mandamus | Command public authority to perform duty |
| Prohibition | Stop lower court from exceeding jurisdiction |
| Certiorari | Transfer/quash case from lower court |
| Quo Warranto | Challenge authority to hold public office |

> Supreme Court → Article 32
>
> High Court → Article 226

# Right to Property

Originally:

Fundamental Right

Article:

31

Current Position:

Legal Right

Article:

300A

Removed by:

**44th Constitutional Amendment (1978)**

---

# Restrictions on Fundamental Rights

Rights are **not absolute**.

Reasonable restrictions may be imposed in the interest of:

- Sovereignty
- Security of State
- Public Order
- Decency
- Morality
- Friendly Relations with Foreign States

---

# Suspension of Fundamental Rights

During National Emergency:

- Article 19 is automatically suspended only during an emergency declared on the grounds of **War or External Aggression**.
- Other rights may be affected according to constitutional provisions.

Articles **20 and 21 cannot be suspended**, even during Emergency.

---

# Cause → Effect

### Violation of Fundamental Rights

↓

Citizen approaches Supreme Court.

↓

Article 32 invoked.

↓

Court may issue a writ.

---

### 44th Amendment

↓

Right to Property becomes Legal Right.

---

### 86th Amendment

↓

Right to Education becomes Fundamental Right.

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Article 14 ≠ Article 15

Article 14:
- Equality before Law

Article 15:
- No discrimination

---

### Article 21 ≠ Article 21A

Article 21:
- Life & Liberty

Article 21A:
- Education

---

### Article 32 ≠ Article 226

Article 32:
- Supreme Court
- Fundamental Right

Article 226:
- High Court
- Wider jurisdiction

---

### Article 23 ≠ Article 24

Article 23:
- Human trafficking
- Forced labour

Article 24:
- Child labour

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ Article 21A guarantees education to all citizens.

✅ It guarantees **free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 years**.

---

❌ Right to Property is still a Fundamental Right.

✅ It is now a **Legal Right under Article 300A**.

---

❌ Article 32 empowers the High Courts.

✅ **Article 32** empowers the **Supreme Court**.
**Article 226** empowers the **High Courts**.

---

❌ Bharat Ratna violates Article 18.

✅ **Bharat Ratna is not considered a hereditary title** and does not violate Article 18.

---

❌ All Fundamental Rights are available only to citizens.

✅ Some rights (such as **Articles 14 and 21**) are available to **all persons**, while others (such as **Article 19**) are available **only to citizens**.
