# Chapter 8: Judiciary

> **Part V (Supreme Court):** Articles 124–147
>
> **Part VI (High Courts):** Articles 214–231
>
> **Purpose:** To interpret the Constitution, protect Fundamental Rights, and ensure the Rule of Law.

> **Exam Tip:** AFCAT mostly asks about the **Supreme Court**, **High Courts**, **Judicial Review**, and **Writs**.

# Supreme Court

## Constitutional Position

- Highest Court of India.
- Guardian of the Constitution.
- Final Court of Appeal.
- Protector of Fundamental Rights.

Seat:

**New Delhi**

Established:

**28 January 1950**

---

## Composition

Consists of:

- Chief Justice of India (CJI)
- Other Judges

> The **number of judges is determined by Parliament** and may change through legislation.

---

## Appointment

Appointed by:

**President of India**

Based on:

**Collegium System**

---

## Qualifications

A judge must:

- Be a citizen of India.
- Have served as a High Court Judge for at least **5 years**, **or**
- Have been an advocate of a High Court for at least **10 years**, **or**
- Be, in the President's opinion, a distinguished jurist.

---

## Retirement Age

**65 years**

---

# Removal of Supreme Court Judges

Method:

Removal by the President after an address passed by **both Houses of Parliament**.

Grounds:

- Proved Misbehaviour
- Incapacity

> Commonly referred to as **Impeachment of Judges**, although the Constitution uses a different procedure from the President's impeachment.

---

# Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court

---

## Original Jurisdiction

Article:

**131**

Deals with disputes involving:

- Union Government
- State Governments

---

## Appellate Jurisdiction

Hears appeals from:

- High Courts

---

## Advisory Jurisdiction

Article:

**143**

President may seek the Court's opinion on questions of law.

---

## Review Jurisdiction

Article:

**137**

Supreme Court may review its own judgments.

---

# Judicial Review

Power to examine whether:

- Laws
- Executive actions

are constitutional.

If unconstitutional:

They can be declared void.

Borrowed from:

**United States Constitution**

---

# Judicial Activism

Meaning:

Active role of the judiciary in protecting constitutional values.

Examples:

- Environmental protection
- Human rights
- Public Interest Litigation (PIL)

---

# Public Interest Litigation (PIL)

Allows:

Any public-spirited citizen

to approach the Court for public welfare.

Purpose:

- Protect rights of disadvantaged sections.
- Promote justice.

---

# Writ Jurisdiction

## Supreme Court

Article:

**32**

Can issue five writs.

---

## Five Writs

| Writ | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Habeas Corpus | Produce detained person before Court |
| Mandamus | Command public authority to perform duty |
| Prohibition | Stop lower court from exceeding jurisdiction |
| Certiorari | Transfer/Quash lower court decision |
| Quo Warranto | Challenge authority to hold public office |

---

# High Courts

Articles:

214–231

Highest Court of the State.

Each State has:

One High Court.

(Some High Courts have jurisdiction over more than one State or Union Territory.)

---

## Appointment

Judges appointed by:

President

---

## Retirement Age

**62 years**

---

# Writ Jurisdiction

Article:

**226**

High Courts can issue writs:

- For Fundamental Rights.
- For other legal rights.

> **High Courts have wider writ jurisdiction than the Supreme Court.**

---

# Subordinate Courts

Articles:

233–237

Include:

- District Courts
- Sessions Courts
- Civil Courts
- Criminal Courts

Work under:

High Courts.

---

# Independence of Judiciary

Ensured by:

- Security of tenure
- Fixed salaries
- Difficult removal process
- Independent functioning
- Separation from Executive

---

# Important Supreme Court Cases

## Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)

Established:

**Basic Structure Doctrine**

Meaning:

Parliament can amend the Constitution but **cannot alter its Basic Structure**.

---

## Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)

Expanded the scope of:

**Article 21**

Held that:

Right to Life means more than mere animal existence.

---

## Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)

Held:

Harmony between:

- Fundamental Rights
- Directive Principles

Both are part of the Constitution's Basic Structure.

---

## S. R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

Held:

- Federalism is part of the Basic Structure.
- President's Rule is subject to judicial review.

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Article 32 ≠ Article 226

Article 32:
- Supreme Court
- Fundamental Rights only

Article 226:
- High Court
- Fundamental Rights + Other Legal Rights

---

### Judicial Review ≠ Judicial Activism

Judicial Review:
- Tests constitutionality.

Judicial Activism:
- Active interpretation for public welfare.

---

### PIL ≠ Writ Petition

PIL:
- Public interest.
- Any public-spirited person can approach the court (subject to judicial guidelines).

Writ Petition:
- Usually filed for enforcement of legal or Fundamental Rights.

---

### Retirement Age

Supreme Court:
- 65 Years

High Court:
- 62 Years

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ High Courts have the same writ jurisdiction as the Supreme Court.

✅ **High Courts have wider writ jurisdiction** because they can enforce **both Fundamental Rights and other legal rights**.

---

❌ Supreme Court judges retire at 62 years.

✅ **Supreme Court judges retire at 65 years.**
**High Court judges retire at 62 years.**

---

❌ Judicial Review was borrowed from the United Kingdom.

✅ It was inspired primarily by the **United States Constitution**.

---

❌ The Basic Structure Doctrine was established in the Minerva Mills case.

✅ It was established in the **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** case.
**Minerva Mills (1980)** strengthened and reaffirmed the doctrine.

---

❌ PIL can only be filed by the affected person.

✅ A **Public Interest Litigation** may be filed by a **public-spirited person** for the benefit of the public or disadvantaged groups.
