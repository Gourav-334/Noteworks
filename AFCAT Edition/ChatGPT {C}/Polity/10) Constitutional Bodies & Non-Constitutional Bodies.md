# Chapter 10: Constitutional Bodies & Non-Constitutional Bodies

> **Purpose:** Understand the important constitutional and statutory institutions of India frequently asked in AFCAT.

> **Exam Tip:** AFCAT generally asks:
> - Which Article created the body?
> - Who appoints its members?
> - Main function.
> - Whether it is Constitutional, Statutory or Non-Constitutional.

---

# Classification

## Constitutional Bodies

Created directly by the Constitution.

Examples:

- Election Commission of India (ECI)
- Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)
- Finance Commission
- Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)
- Attorney General of India
- National Commission for SCs
- National Commission for STs

---

## Statutory Bodies

Created by an Act of Parliament.

Examples:

- Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)
- Central Information Commission (CIC)
- National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)
- Lokpal
- National Green Tribunal (NGT)

---

## Non-Constitutional / Executive Bodies

Created by Executive Resolution or Government decision.

Examples:

- NITI Aayog
- National Development Council *(historical)*
- National Security Council

---

# Election Commission of India (ECI)

## Constitutional Position

Article:

**324**

Type:

Constitutional Body

---

## Composition

- Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)
- Election Commissioners

Appointed by:

President of India

---

## Functions

- Conducts elections to:
  - Lok Sabha
  - Rajya Sabha
  - State Legislatures
  - President
  - Vice-President

- Supervises electoral rolls.
- Enforces the Model Code of Conduct.

---

# Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)

## Constitutional Position

Articles:

**315–323**

Type:

Constitutional Body

---

## Appointment

Chairman and Members appointed by:

President

---

## Functions

- Conducts Civil Services Examination.
- Advises Government on recruitment.
- Advises on promotions and disciplinary matters.

---

# Finance Commission

## Constitutional Position

Article:

**280**

Type:

Constitutional Body

---

## Appointment

Constituted by:

President

Every:

**5 Years**

---

## Functions

Recommends distribution of:

- Taxes
- Grants

between:

- Union
- States

---

# Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)

## Constitutional Position

Articles:

**148–151**

Type:

Constitutional Body

---

## Appointment

By:

President

---

## Functions

- Audits accounts of:
  - Union Government
  - State Governments
- Ensures financial accountability.

Known as:

**Guardian of the Public Purse**

---

# Attorney General of India

## Constitutional Position

Article:

**76**

Type:

Constitutional Office

Highest Law Officer of India.

---

## Appointment

By:

President

---

## Functions

- Legal advisor to the Government of India.
- Appears before the Supreme Court.
- Can participate in Parliament but **cannot vote**.

---

# National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)

Article:

**338**

Type:

Constitutional Body

Functions:

- Safeguards interests of Scheduled Castes.
- Investigates complaints.

---

# National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)

Article:

**338A**

Type:

Constitutional Body

Functions:

- Welfare of Scheduled Tribes.
- Reports to the President.

---

# Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)

Type:

Statutory Body

Established:

**2003**

Main Function:

- Prevent corruption.
- Vigilance over Central Government departments.

---

# Central Information Commission (CIC)

Type:

Statutory Body

Established under:

**Right to Information Act, 2005**

Functions:

- Ensures implementation of RTI Act.

---

# National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

Type:

Statutory Body

Established:

**1993**

Under:

**Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**

Functions:

- Protect human rights.
- Investigate violations.

---

# Lokpal

Type:

Statutory Body

Established under:

**Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013**

Function:

- Investigates corruption against public officials.

---

# National Green Tribunal (NGT)

Type:

Statutory Body

Established:

**2010**

Purpose:

- Speedy disposal of environmental cases.

---

# NITI Aayog

Type:

Non-Constitutional Body

Established:

**1 January 2015**

Replaced:

**Planning Commission**

Chairperson:

**Prime Minister**

Functions:

- Policy formulation.
- Cooperative Federalism.
- Long-term planning.

---

# National Security Council (NSC)

Type:

Executive Body

Chairperson:

Prime Minister

Purpose:

- National Security.
- Strategic Policy.
- Defence Coordination.

---

# National Development Council (NDC)

Type:

Executive Body *(historically significant)*

Purpose:

- Coordination of national planning.
- Worked alongside the Planning Commission.

> **Its role has largely ceased after the replacement of the Planning Commission by NITI Aayog.**

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Election Commission ≠ State Election Commission

Election Commission of India:
- Article 324
- Parliament
- President
- Vice-President
- State Legislatures

State Election Commission:
- State Body
- Panchayats
- Municipalities

---

### UPSC ≠ Finance Commission

UPSC:
- Recruitment

Finance Commission:
- Tax Distribution

---

### Attorney General ≠ Advocate General

Attorney General:
- Union
- Article 76

Advocate General:
- State
- Article 165

---

### CAG ≠ Finance Commission

CAG:
- Audit

Finance Commission:
- Tax Sharing

---

### Constitutional ≠ Statutory ≠ Executive

Constitutional:
- Constitution

Statutory:
- Act of Parliament

Executive:
- Government Resolution

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ NITI Aayog is a Constitutional Body.

✅ **NITI Aayog is a Non-Constitutional (Executive) Body.**

---

❌ CAG distributes taxes between the Union and States.

✅ The **Finance Commission** recommends tax distribution.
The **CAG audits government accounts**.

---

❌ UPSC conducts elections.

✅ The **Election Commission** conducts elections.
**UPSC** conducts recruitment examinations.

---

❌ The Attorney General is a Constitutional Body.

✅ The **Attorney General is a Constitutional Office** under **Article 76**, not a constitutional body.

---

❌ NHRC is a Constitutional Body.

✅ The **NHRC is a Statutory Body**, established under the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**.
