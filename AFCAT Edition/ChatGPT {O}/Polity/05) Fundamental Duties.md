# What are Fundamental Duties?

Fundamental Duties are:

- Moral obligations of citizens.
- Not enforceable by courts.
- Intended to promote patriotism, discipline and responsible citizenship.

> Unlike Fundamental Rights, they are **available only to citizens**.

---

# Historical Background

The original Constitution (1950):

- Had **Fundamental Rights**.
- Had **Directive Principles of State Policy**.
- **Did NOT have Fundamental Duties.**

The **Swaran Singh Committee (1976)** recommended the inclusion of Fundamental Duties.

Based on this recommendation, the **42nd Constitutional Amendment** inserted **Part IVA (Article 51A)**.

---

# Article 51A

Contains the Fundamental Duties of every citizen.

Originally:

**10 Duties**

Present:

**11 Duties**

---

# The Eleven Fundamental Duties

## (a)

To abide by the Constitution and respect:

- Its ideals
- Institutions
- National Flag
- National Anthem

---

## (b)

To cherish and follow the noble ideals of the Freedom Struggle.

---

## (c)

To uphold and protect:

- Sovereignty
- Unity
- Integrity of India

---

## (d)

To defend the country and render national service when called upon.

---

## (e)

To promote:

- Harmony
- Spirit of common brotherhood

and renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

---

## (f)

To value and preserve the rich heritage of India's composite culture.

---

## (g)

To protect and improve:

- Environment
- Forests
- Lakes
- Rivers
- Wildlife

and have compassion for living creatures.

---

## (h)

To develop:

- Scientific temper
- Humanism
- Spirit of inquiry
- Spirit of reform

---

## (i)

To safeguard public property.

To abjure (avoid) violence.

---

## (j)

To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity.

---

## (k)

Added by:

**86th Constitutional Amendment (2002)**

Duty of every parent or guardian to provide educational opportunities to children aged:

**6–14 years**

# Important Supreme Court Cases

## AIIMS Students' Union Case (2001)

Supreme Court observed:

- Fundamental Duties are important for responsible citizenship.
- Though non-justiciable, they help interpret constitutional provisions.

> **AFCAT generally asks only the significance of Fundamental Duties, not detailed case law.**

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Fundamental Duties ≠ DPSPs

Fundamental Duties:
- Duties of Citizens
- Part IVA

DPSPs:
- Duties of State
- Part IV

---

### Article 51A ≠ Article 21A

Article 51A:
- Duty of Citizen

Article 21A:
- Right to Education

---

### 42nd Amendment ≠ 86th Amendment

42nd:
- Fundamental Duties
- Socialist
- Secular
- Integrity

86th:
- Right to Education
- 11th Duty

---

### Rights ≠ Duties

Rights:
- Can be claimed

Duties:
- Should be performed

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ Fundamental Duties were part of the original Constitution.

✅ They were added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976)**.

---

❌ Fundamental Duties are enforceable by courts.

✅ They are **Non-Justiciable** (not directly enforceable).

---

❌ Fundamental Duties were borrowed from the Constitution of Ireland.

✅ They were borrowed from the **Constitution of the USSR**.

---

❌ The Constitution originally contained 11 Fundamental Duties.

✅ It originally contained **10**.
The **11th** was added by the **86th Amendment (2002)**.

---

❌ Article 21A and Article 51A(k) provide the same constitutional provision.

✅ **Article 21A** gives the **child the Right to Education**.
**Article 51A(k)** places a **duty on parents/guardians** to provide educational opportunities to children aged **6–14 years**.
