# Chapter 2: National Income

> **Purpose:** Understand the concepts of GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP, National Income, Per Capita Income and related economic indicators.

> **Exam Tip:** AFCAT mainly asks **full forms, differences, and basic concepts**, not numerical calculations.

---

# Background

**National Income** measures the total value of goods and services produced by an economy during a given period (usually one year).

It helps to measure:

- Economic Growth
- Standard of Living
- Development of a Country

---

# National Income

Meaning:

The total income earned by the residents of a country during one financial year.

Includes:

- Wages
- Rent
- Interest
- Profit

---

# Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

Full Form:

**Gross Domestic Product**

Meaning:

Total value of **final goods and services** produced **within the geographical boundaries of a country** during one year.

Includes:

- Production by Indians
- Production by Foreign Companies located in India

Does **NOT** include:

Production by Indian companies located abroad.

---

# Gross National Product (GNP)

Full Form:

**Gross National Product**

Meaning:

Total value of final goods and services produced by the **citizens (nationals)** of a country, regardless of where they are located.

Formula:

**GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)**

---

# Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)

Meaning:

Income earned by Indian residents from abroad

minus

Income earned by foreign residents in India.

---

# Net Domestic Product (NDP)

Full Form:

**Net Domestic Product**

Meaning:

GDP after deducting depreciation.

Formula:

**NDP = GDP − Depreciation**

---

# Net National Product (NNP)

Full Form:

**Net National Product**

Meaning:

GNP after deducting depreciation.

Formula:

**NNP = GNP − Depreciation**

---

# National Income (At Factor Cost)

Traditionally:

**National Income = NNP at Factor Cost**

> Modern national accounts use updated terminology, but AFCAT generally follows the traditional definition.

---

# Depreciation

Meaning:

Loss in value of fixed assets due to:

- Wear and Tear
- Age
- Obsolescence

Examples:

- Machines
- Buildings
- Equipment

---

# Market Price vs Factor Cost

## Market Price (MP)

Includes:

- Indirect Taxes

Less:

- Subsidies

---

## Factor Cost (FC)

Income actually received by:

- Labour
- Land
- Capital
- Entrepreneurs

---

# Factor Cost vs Market Price

| Factor Cost | Market Price |
|--------------|--------------|
| Excludes Indirect Taxes | Includes Indirect Taxes |
| Includes Subsidies | Excludes Subsidies |

---

# Nominal GDP

Calculated using:

**Current Year Prices**

Affected by:

Inflation.

---

# Real GDP

Calculated using:

**Constant (Base Year) Prices**

Shows:

Actual increase in production.

---

# Nominal GDP vs Real GDP

| Nominal GDP | Real GDP |
|--------------|-----------|
| Current Prices | Constant Prices |
| Includes Inflation | Excludes Inflation Effect |
| Less Accurate for Growth | Better Growth Indicator |

---

# GDP Deflator

Measures:

Overall price rise in the economy.

Formula:

**GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100**

> AFCAT generally asks only the concept, not the formula.

---

# Per Capita Income

Meaning:

Average income per person.

Formula:

**Per Capita Income = National Income ÷ Population**

Importance:

Used to compare:

- Living Standards
- Economic Development

---

# Disposable Income

Meaning:

Income available after paying:

Taxes.

Can be:

- Spent
- Saved

---

# Personal Income

Income actually received by individuals.

Includes:

- Salaries
- Interest
- Pension
- Transfers

---

# Transfer Payments

Payments received without providing goods or services.

Examples:

- Pension
- Scholarship
- Unemployment Benefits
- Subsidies

These are **not included** in current production.

---

# Value Added

Meaning:

Increase in value during production.

Example:

Wood:

₹100

↓

Furniture:

₹300

Value Added:

₹200

> GDP counts **value added**, avoiding **double counting**.

---

# Double Counting

Occurs when:

The value of intermediate goods is counted multiple times.

Avoided by:

- Counting only final goods.
- Using value-added method.

---

# Methods of Measuring National Income

## Product Method

Measures:

Value of goods & services produced.

---

## Income Method

Measures:

Income earned by factors of production.

---

## Expenditure Method

Measures:

Total expenditure on final goods and services.

---

# Cause → Effect

### Production Increases

↓

GDP Increases

↓

Economic Growth

---

### Inflation Increases

↓

Nominal GDP Increases

↓

Real GDP may remain unchanged

---

### Population Increases Faster than Income

↓

Per Capita Income Falls

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### GDP ≠ GNP

GDP:
- Geographic Boundaries

GNP:
- Nationals

---

### Gross ≠ Net

Gross:
- Before Depreciation

Net:
- After Depreciation

---

### Nominal ≠ Real GDP

Nominal:
- Current Prices

Real:
- Constant Prices

---

### Market Price ≠ Factor Cost

Market Price:
- Includes Indirect Taxes

Factor Cost:
- Income received by Factors of Production

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ GDP includes production by Indian companies abroad.

✅ **GDP** includes production **within India's borders**, regardless of ownership.
Production by Indian companies abroad is reflected in **GNP**, not GDP.

---

❌ Gross means after deducting depreciation.

✅ **Gross** means **before depreciation**.
**Net** means **after depreciation**.

---

❌ Real GDP is calculated using current prices.

✅ **Real GDP** is calculated using **constant (base year) prices**, removing the effect of inflation.

---

❌ Per Capita Income measures the total income of a country.

✅ **Per Capita Income** is the **average income per person**.

---

❌ Intermediate goods are fully counted in GDP.

✅ GDP measures the value of **final goods and services** or **value added**, preventing **double counting**.
