# Chapter 4: Inflation & Monetary Policy

> **Purpose:** Understand inflation, deflation, monetary policy and the tools used by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to control the money supply.

> **Exam Tip:** AFCAT frequently asks about **Inflation, Repo Rate, Reverse Repo Rate, CRR, SLR, Open Market Operations (OMO), and Monetary Policy**.

---

# Background

An economy requires:

- Stable Prices
- Controlled Inflation
- Balanced Growth

The institution mainly responsible for controlling inflation in India is:

**Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**

through:

**Monetary Policy**

---

# Inflation

Meaning:

A **continuous rise** in the general price level of goods and services over time.

Effects:

- Purchasing power decreases.
- Cost of living increases.

---

# Causes of Inflation

- Increase in money supply.
- High demand.
- Low production.
- Rising production costs.
- Supply chain disruptions.

---

# Types of Inflation

## Demand-Pull Inflation

Occurs when:

Demand > Supply

Example:

High consumer spending.

---

## Cost-Push Inflation

Occurs due to:

Increase in production costs.

Examples:

- Fuel price increase.
- Wage increase.
- Raw material shortage.

---

## Creeping Inflation

Slow increase.

Usually:

Below **3% per year** *(approximate textbook classification)*.

---

## Walking Inflation

Moderate increase.

---

## Running Inflation

Rapid increase.

---

## Hyperinflation

Extremely high inflation.

Currency loses value rapidly.

Historical Example:

Germany (1923), Zimbabwe (2000s).

---

# Deflation

Meaning:

Continuous fall in the general price level.

Effects:

- Lower demand.
- Lower profits.
- Higher unemployment.

---

# Disinflation

Meaning:

Inflation is still positive,

but

its **rate slows down**.

Example:

Inflation falls from:

8%

↓

5%

Prices are still increasing, but more slowly.

---

# Stagflation

Combination of:

- High Inflation
- High Unemployment
- Low Economic Growth

One of the most difficult economic situations to manage.

---

# Purchasing Power

Meaning:

Quantity of goods and services that money can buy.

Inflation:

↓

Purchasing Power decreases.

---

# Monetary Policy

Meaning:

Policy of the **Reserve Bank of India** to regulate:

- Money Supply
- Credit
- Interest Rates

Objectives:

- Price Stability
- Economic Growth
- Financial Stability

---

# Quantitative (General) Tools

These affect the **overall money supply**.

---

## Repo Rate

Meaning:

Rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks against government securities.

### Repo Rate ↑

↓

Loans become costlier.

↓

Borrowing decreases.

↓

Inflation may decrease.

---

### Repo Rate ↓

↓

Loans become cheaper.

↓

Borrowing increases.

↓

Economic activity increases.

---

## Reverse Repo Rate

Meaning:

Rate at which RBI borrows money from commercial banks.

### Reverse Repo ↑

↓

Banks deposit more money with RBI.

↓

Money supply decreases.

---

# Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)

Meaning:

Percentage of deposits that banks must keep with RBI as **cash**.

No interest is paid by RBI on CRR balances.

---

### CRR ↑

↓

Less money available for lending.

↓

Money Supply decreases.

---

### CRR ↓

↓

Banks can lend more.

↓

Money Supply increases.

---

# Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)

Meaning:

Percentage of deposits that banks must maintain in the form of:

- Cash
- Gold
- Approved Government Securities

Maintained by:

Banks themselves.

---

### SLR ↑

↓

Lending decreases.

↓

Money Supply decreases.

---

# Bank Rate

Meaning:

Long-term rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks.

Used as a monetary policy tool.

---

# Open Market Operations (OMO)

Meaning:

Buying and selling of Government Securities by RBI.

---

### RBI Buys Securities

↓

Money enters economy.

↓

Liquidity increases.

---

### RBI Sells Securities

↓

Money withdrawn.

↓

Liquidity decreases.

---

# Qualitative (Selective) Tools

Used to control:

Credit for specific sectors.

Examples:

- Margin Requirement
- Moral Suasion
- Credit Rationing
- Direct Action

---

# Margin Requirement

Difference between:

Value of security

and

Loan amount.

Higher Margin:

↓

Less borrowing.

---

# Moral Suasion

RBI persuades banks through:

Advice

rather than legal orders.

---

# Credit Rationing

RBI limits:

Amount of loans banks can provide.

---

# Direct Action

RBI may impose:

Penalties

on banks violating guidelines.

---

# Inflation Measurement in India

## Consumer Price Index (CPI)

Measures:

Retail Inflation.

Reflects prices paid by consumers.

Used by:

RBI for inflation targeting.

---

## Wholesale Price Index (WPI)

Measures:

Wholesale prices.

Tracks inflation at the producer level.

---

# Inflation Target

RBI aims to maintain:

**4% CPI Inflation**

Tolerance Band:

**±2%**

That is:

**2% to 6%**

---

# Liquidity

Meaning:

Availability of money in the economy.

High Liquidity:

More money.

Low Liquidity:

Less money.

---

# Cause → Effect

### Repo Rate ↑

↓

# Easy-to-Confuse Facts

### Inflation ≠ Deflation

Inflation:
- Prices Rise

Deflation:
- Prices Fall

---

### Inflation ≠ Disinflation

Inflation:
- Prices Continue Rising

Disinflation:
- Prices Still Rise
- But More Slowly

---

### Repo ≠ Bank Rate

Repo:
- Short-term lending against government securities.

Bank Rate:
- Traditional long-term lending rate.

---

### CRR ≠ SLR

CRR:
- Cash
- Kept with RBI

SLR:
- Cash, Gold & Government Securities
- Maintained by Banks

---

### CPI ≠ WPI

CPI:
- Consumer Level

WPI:
- Wholesale Level

---

# Common AFCAT Traps

❌ Inflation increases the purchasing power of money.

✅ **Inflation reduces the purchasing power** because goods and services become more expensive.

---

❌ Repo Rate is the rate at which banks lend money to customers.

✅ **Repo Rate** is the rate at which the **RBI lends money to commercial banks**.

---

❌ CRR and SLR are the same.

✅ **CRR** is **cash kept with the RBI**.
**SLR** is **cash, gold and approved government securities maintained by banks**.

---

❌ CPI measures wholesale inflation.

✅ **CPI** measures **retail (consumer) inflation**.
**WPI** measures **wholesale inflation**.

---

❌ When RBI buys government securities, money supply decreases.

✅ When **RBI buys government securities**, it injects money into the economy, **increasing liquidity and money supply**.
